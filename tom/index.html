The Fix

What I Would Do Instead

  • Fill the gap in the jumping progression. Stage 14 of the program doesn't clear you for running. Before running, you need to get through single-leg pogos, skipping, and bounding. The force going through the joints in those movements is significantly higher. That's where the load tolerance gets built.
  • Give you actual numbers to hit. No physio has given you a strength benchmark yet. As a starting point, your calves alone need a three-rep max calf raise at a minimum of 80% of your bodyweight in external load. That's more than most people are doing. If there's a deficit there, that's part of why things are creeping back in when you start running.
  • Get the rehab inside your training, not before it. Doing your physio work as a warm-up before a Hyrox session is not replicating the demands. The rehab should be your training, at least for now. Split squats, RDLs, the calf work. All built into a proper session with the right order and the right load.
  • Simplify what you're doing. Nail the shin first. Get back to running. Then the Hyrox, then the marathon. You can do all of those things, but trying to do them all at the same time is what's making the injury harder to manage.

Once you can see the numbers and you're hitting them, it's not "I think I'm ready." It's "I'm ready." That's what you said you wanted from this.

Structure

Conditioning

  • Off-feet zone two on Saturday. Bike, ski, row. No impact
  • The Hyrox-style sessions stay if they're not cutting into recovery. We adjust as we go
  • Upper body mixed in around all of it
